The 300-game result changes the recommendation substantially. You now have a validated learning improvement, a validated search-speed improvement, and a credible—though riskier—style improvement. This is no longer a project where the only positive evidence comes from inference-time search.

I would reopen improvement work, but as a bounded campaign built around the methods that just worked, rather than another broad search through training algorithms.

My priority order would be:

    Unify and audit the actual policy implementations.
    Test the improved student as both a player and a search component.
    Run one controlled round of continued search distillation and genuine aggregation.
    Improve λ10’s risk management rather than simply increasing its style weight.
    Confirm promising configurations on longer horizons.

1. First, update the interpretation of these results
The student is a decisive success—not merely an encouraging signal

The +468-piece improvement over 300 paired games is strong evidence for the student checkpoint under the sweep harness. It establishes that:

    this architecture can internalize useful information from search;
    hard-argmax CE can provide a substantial improvement;
    the full-split fitting diagnostics were inadequate as a gameplay gate;
    poor teacher reproduction does not imply poor policy improvement.

However, I would slightly amend this explanation:

    “The flipped quarter of decisions carries large survival value.”

That is plausible, but not established. Training also changed formerly agreed decisions, score margins, and behavior on states outside the bank. The demonstrated conclusion is:

    The update extracted enough useful structure from the teacher to produce a much stronger policy, despite imperfect—and in some respects worse—ranking fidelity.

That distinction matters when deciding what to optimize next. You should not assume that maximizing agree_dis is now the correct objective.
Top-k is promising, but “champion-equivalent” remains horizon-specific

All four variants surviving 20/20 games is excellent evidence for their practical usefulness. It does not establish equal failure rates over longer games.

Also, this sentence no longer follows:

    “Diverse shortlist, not v1 top-k” still stands.

Both naive and diverse shortlists passed. Diversity is a reasonable precaution, but its advantage over naive shortlisting has not been demonstrated. Similarly, the difference between 6 and 15 incidental Tetrises is not a compelling reason to reject a faster survival policy. Test style preservation using a genuinely stylish configuration instead.
λ10 should be treated as a measured trade-off, not forced into “safe/unsafe”

The style increase is now convincing. Its inventory behavior is clearly different from λ20’s accumulation failure.

But the observed death at 2685 pieces remains evidence, even if the fresh 20-game panel subsequently has zero deaths. With zero failures in 20 games, the one-sided 95% upper bound on per-game failure probability is still about 14% at that horizon.

I would describe λ10 as:

    A sustained style improvement with an observed additional long-horizon failure mode.

That is enough to justify improving it. It need not first be certified indistinguishable from the survival champion.
2. Highest priority: resolve the policy identity problem

The student’s sweep-versus-aggregation discrepancy is now more important, not less.

The promotion remains valid for the sweep policy: both weights were compared in that harness. But you need to know which policy was actually promoted before building another training round around it.
A bounded audit

For one tape where the student’s trajectories diverge:

    Run both implementations from exactly the same board and queue.
    Compare candidate sets using canonical identifiers:
        placement geometry;
        resulting board;
        lockout status.
    Compare:
        encoded board tensors;
        current/preview inputs;
        scores aligned by canonical candidate;
        masks;
        tie-breaking;
        selected action.
    Stop at the first difference, not the first death.

Do the same for visualization when convenient, but prioritize the training/evaluation discrepancy.

The durable fix is one shared policy implementation with adapters for collection, evaluation, and visualization. Keep the promoted configuration versioned; do not silently “correct” it.
Why this has unusually high expected value

The project has repeatedly paid for experiments whose conclusions were later overturned by differences between nominally equivalent policies.

The next distillation campaign needs to answer:

    Is the student being trained and deployed with the same input semantics?

In particular, verify true-preview versus duplicated-piece slots in the actual training and deployment tensors. The previous audit of the incumbent’s weak response to preview substitution did not establish that a newly trained student would also be insensitive.
3. Next: run a small weights × search crossover

This is my highest-value new performance experiment. You have improved both weights and search, but have not measured their interaction.

Use a fixed development panel and a longer cap—perhaps 3000 pieces—because 1500 increasingly hides differences.

A compact matrix would be:
Weights	Inference	Purpose
snapshot9	full 2-ply	Established reference
fullsplit	1-ply	New fast baseline
snapshot9	beam5 or beam8	Established cheaper search
fullsplit	same beam5 or beam8	Do stronger weights permit cheaper search?
fullsplit	full 2-ply	Does the student improve search evaluation?

A modest paired development panel is sufficient for triage; it is not a final reliability claim.

Record:

    survival distribution and cap rate;
    early failures;
    wall time per decision, including upper percentiles;
    occupancy/height excursions;
    reason for death.

Do not spend much interpretive effort on incidental Tetrises in these bonus-free policies.
Why this is valuable

The student may:

    improve both greedy play and search;
    improve greedy play but not search;
    permit a much narrower shortlist;
    hurt search despite better greedy behavior.

All four are possible.

Search distillation changes what the scalar scores represent. A model trained to rank one-step candidates like a two-step teacher is not automatically a better leaf value function. The interaction must be measured.

For the initial crossover, use one model consistently at both plies. Do not mix snapshot9 root values with student leaf values in an additive score without checking their scale and semantics.
4. A particularly attractive speed experiment: student proposals, incumbent evaluation

There is another way to exploit the student that avoids requiring it to be a good leaf evaluator.

Use the student to propose promising roots, but score the expanded roots with the unchanged snapshot9 2-ply teacher.

For example, build a small union of:

    top roots under snapshot9;
    top roots under the student;
    one or two geometry-based safety candidates.

Then fully expand only that union.

This cleanly separates two roles:

    Student: predicts which actions deserve expensive consideration.
    Teacher: makes the final decision using its existing scoring function.

Cheap offline test first

On a mixed state bank, measure:

    fraction of states where the shortlist contains the full teacher’s winner;
    missed-winner regret;
    avoidable-dead omissions;
    number of unique shortlisted roots;
    measured inference cost.

Include student-visited and high-stack states, not only the original bank.

Then take only the best one or two shortlist designs into closed-loop evaluation.

The student is explicitly trained to predict the search teacher’s choices, so proposal generation is a particularly natural use of its learned information. It could outperform a plain v1 shortlist even if student-weighted 2-ply search does not.
5. Reopen training with one controlled iteration—not a new algorithm sweep

The simplest productive question is now:

    Does more of the successful learning process continue to help, and does student-state aggregation make it better?

I would compare two arms, both starting from the promoted fullsplit checkpoint.
Arm A: continued fitting on the existing bank

Continue the successful hard-argmax CE recipe with several saved checkpoints.

This answers whether the first run stopped while there was still useful improvement available. The earlier argument that validation agreement had plateaued no longer justifies ruling this out.
Arm B: equal-budget fitting with student-state aggregation

Collect states from the promoted student using the canonical playing policy, label them with the frozen snapshot9 full-search teacher, and mix them with the original bank.

Use approximately the same optimizer-update budget as Arm A so the comparison addresses new data versus additional optimization, rather than both simultaneously.

Include:

    ordinary student trajectories;
    high-stack and recovery states;
    disagreements;
    a substantial share of ordinary agreements, to resist forgetting.

If you oversample particular categories, report metrics on an unweighted validation distribution as well.
Keep the teacher fixed initially

Do not simultaneously change:

    teacher weights;
    search depth;
    training loss;
    input semantics;
    and data distribution.

The current teacher has already demonstrated that it can improve the student. Exploit that result before introducing another moving target.
Change the gates

Use offline metrics for diagnosis:

    teacher regret;
    avoidable-dead choices;
    corrections versus newly introduced disagreements;
    performance stratified by stack condition.

But use a reused gameplay development panel to decide which checkpoints deserve final testing. A checkpoint should not need 95% disagreement agreement before being allowed to play.

If one more iteration improves convincingly, then run a second aggregation round and replicate the recipe across training schedules/seeds. If it does not, you have a useful stopping point without launching a large campaign.

I would not make soft targets, residual learning, or a new architecture the first experiment now. They remain reasonable alternatives, but hard CE has just earned the right to be the control.
6. For style, target the backlog-spike failure mode

λ10’s problem is no longer “cannot sustain setups.” It appears to be:

    Usually stable setup-seeking behavior, with occasional excursions from which its continued style pressure fails to recover.

That suggests a low-dimensional controller rather than a larger λ.
First build a compact failure-and-recovery bank

Collect states around:

    the λ10 death at 2685;
    large λ10 spikes that successfully recover;
    comparable champion excursions.

Include some lead-up, not just the final nearly dead boards.

Ask:

    When does λ10 first choose differently from the survival policy?
    When would switching to pure survival still permit recovery?
    Does the survival policy recover from the same λ10-generated state, or only avoid reaching it?

That last distinction is important. A rescue controller is useful only if it intervenes before the state becomes unrecoverable.
Then test a simple hysteretic style schedule

For example:

    normal conditions: λ10 + completion bonus;
    elevated risk: reduce setup pressure;
    severe risk: temporarily use the bonus-free survival policy;
    restore style only after reaching a lower recovery threshold.

Risk could initially use occupancy, top height, holes, or placement availability. Keep the parameter count small.

Hysteresis matters: without separate entry and exit thresholds, the policy can repeatedly build and dismantle the same plan.

This is not a survival guarantee. It is a specific, testable intervention against an observed failure mode.
Evaluate a Pareto frontier

Track:

    Tetrises per piece;
    long-horizon failures;
    high-stack dwell time;
    recovery from excursions;
    how often the controller disables style.

The objective should be “retain most of λ10’s style while reducing catastrophic excursions,” not “never show a positive occupancy slope.”

And test λ10 with cheap search separately. The bonus-free shortlist results do not establish that the same shortlist preserves setup moves.
7. What I would defer

Despite the renewed optimism, I would still defer:

    training a new architecture from scratch;
    a broad PPO/TD algorithm campaign;
    deeper stochastic search on every move;
    elaborate well-column switching;
    distilling style before checking the improved survival student plus direct Phi;
    large final evaluations of every intermediate checkpoint.

The current evidence supports a much narrower program: better weights, cheaper search, one more search-distillation iteration, and targeted style recovery.
Suggested immediate sequence
Order	Experiment	Decision it enables
1	First-divergence audit of sweep vs collector	Establish the exact deployed/trained policy
2	Student weights × search crossover	Identify the best accuracy–compute configuration
3	Student-assisted shortlist coverage, then gameplay	Exploit learned search knowledge cheaply
4	Continued CE vs equal-budget student-state aggregation	Test whether the learning improvement compounds
5	λ10 failure/recovery analysis and hysteretic fallback	Improve style without blindly increasing risk
6	Fresh long-horizon confirmation of the best configurations	Support promotion claims

I would keep a small demo available as a sanity check, even while postponing the polished demo phase. Watching representative failures can expose problems that aggregate metrics miss.

The central update is that the project has now demonstrated successful search-to-network learning—not merely failed to disprove it. That makes another bounded improvement campaign well justified. The strongest next move is not to search for a radically new idea, but to determine whether the successful teacher–student step can be repeated and whether the resulting student lets you spend search only where it pays.
