I would change the priority order. Keeping snapshot9 + full 2-ply as the reference is sensible, but the evidence does not yet justify making input representation the next major project.

The most promising next step is to produce a genuinely Tetris-seeking policy through dense setup incentives or explicit multi-move scaffolding, then use that policy to generate training data. I would move §14.4’s “fallback” scaffolding branch ahead of representation changes.

Your experiments establish that the particular bonuses and training recipes tested did not work. They do not establish that the network cannot represent well-building.
1. What the results actually suggest
The well bonus was still a sparse reward

Your setup score requires:

    A clean well at least six cells deep;
    No holes;
    No second substantial well.

That rewards an almost-completed setup, not the process of constructing one. The availability result—only 1.8% of visited states have any qualifying candidate—is exactly what I would expect when a flat-stacking policy is offered that reward.

It does not close the search-bonus branch. It closes something narrower:

    A shallow search with a thresholded reward for an already-developed well cannot reliably bootstrap well-building from flat-stack states.

The untested alternative is to reward useful intermediate configurations at depths one, two, three, and four, rather than waiting until six. A six-deep well is not even necessary for a first Tetris: four suitably filled rows and a reachable vertical I placement suffice.
Added channels supply structure, not motivation

Height, holes, and wells are derivable from occupancy. Explicit channels may improve sample efficiency or make a small model easier to train, but they add an inductive bias, not a new objective.

If you:

    Add height/well channels;
    Train the new model to reproduce snapshot9;
    Apply the same sparse well bonus;

there is no strong reason to expect different behavior. Successful distillation should initially reproduce the same flat-stacking preferences.

Representation becomes a higher-priority suspect if you have good style targets that the model cannot fit or generalize. You do not yet have those targets.
Failed distillation does not establish that lookahead cannot be distilled

The §13.3 explanation—“memorizes picks, not reasons”—is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated mechanism.

If the student receives the same decision information as the teacher, the teacher’s two-ply action is a function of that information. In principle, a policy can approximate that function without performing explicit search.

Your experiment tested a particular combination of:

    4,000 frozen states;
    400 updates;
    A particular architecture and loss;
    A different deployment distribution once the student starts acting.

That can fail because of underfitting, distribution shift, feature limitations, or training details. It does not close search distillation as a method. More importantly, distilling a flat-playing teacher was never a direct test of learning Tetris style.
2. First fix two misleading “safety” assumptions
“Survivors-only” is not a survival guarantee

Excluding moves that immediately lose—or lose within two plies—does not protect against moves that create an unrecoverable position ten pieces later.

Likewise:

    Freezing the survival network protects its weights, not the combined policy.
    A value-margin veto is a useful heuristic, not a certified safety constraint.
    Subtracting the style-score mean does not bound its influence. Within a candidate set, it leaves every pairwise score difference unchanged.

The Phase-A collapse demonstrates this distinction directly.

Also, §14’s α=0 baseline caps only 26/30 games, whereas the champion full-two-ply configuration caps 30/30. That appears to be a different playing configuration. Before further experiments, make the style harness reproduce snapshot9 + full 2-ply at zero style weight, including action agreement on a fixed bank.

A useful engineering contract is:

    Style weight zero must reproduce the champion’s chosen actions, terminal handling, and game results exactly.

Survival is strongly demonstrated, not proven endless

The long games are excellent evidence of robustness. They are not an indefinite-survival guarantee, especially after changing the policy.

For perspective, observing zero failures in 30 independent games still permits a roughly 9.5% per-game failure probability at a one-sided 95% confidence bound—and that statement only concerns the tested horizon.

Keep cap-300 tests for cheap style screening, but validate promising style policies on long games and difficult piece sequences.
3. What I would try next, in order
A. Build a simple, explicit Tetris-stacking controller

This is my highest-priority experiment because it answers the most important missing question:

    Can this simulator, reachability model, and shallow search produce sustained Tetrises with a straightforward constructive policy?

For example:

    Reserve a chosen side column as the intended well.
    Prefer filling the other nine columns without holes.
    Reward rows becoming complete except for the well cell.
    Reward a reachable vertical-I channel.
    Discourage covering or obstructing that channel.
    Switch to recovery play when height or accessibility becomes dangerous.
    Clear the well when an I is available and a Tetris is reachable.

Use actual ARS/20G reachability rather than column heights alone. A geometrically attractive board can still be awkward or unusable under ARS. Do not assume the two sides are interchangeable.

This can be a small finite-state controller:

BUILD → CASH OUT → BUILD, with RECOVER available throughout.

It need not be elegant or outperform the final learned policy. It is a diagnostic and a teacher. If it produces a substantial Tetris rate, you have shown that new representation is not a prerequisite for obtaining the behavior.
B. Replace the thresholded bonus with dense, designated-well features

The key is to reward partial progress from the states you actually visit.

Candidate features include:
Feature	Purpose
Number of rows filled except for the designated well	Direct progress toward a Tetris
Partial occupancy of those rows outside the well	Signal before rows are almost complete
Clean well depth, with credit starting below four	Avoid the current availability problem
Obstructions in the well	Preserve the I channel
Holes and covered holes outside the well	Avoid counterfeit “progress”
Surface roughness outside the well	Keep the working stack usable
Actual reachable I-clear opportunities	Respect 20G geometry
Stack height and legal-placement diversity	Penalize brittle setups

Initially use a fixed designated well, not “whichever column currently looks deepest.” Otherwise the policy can collect apparent progress by switching its notion of the target well.

Also avoid indefinitely rewarding deeper wells. Reward progress toward a usable four-line setup, then saturate or add risk penalties. An unbounded depth bonus can teach the bot to hoard a dangerous stack instead of clearing it.

A practical initial score is:

[ S(a)=Q_{\text{base,2ply}}(s,a) +\lambda,\Phi(s_a) +\beta,R_{\text{clear}}(s,a), ]

where (\Phi) is a dense setup potential, not the current all-or-nothing detector.

This is an experimental heuristic, not a safety guarantee. Start with a small feature set and inspect the resulting trajectories.

Important: retain an explicit reward for completing the Tetris. Clearing a good well destroys the setup features; a setup-only objective can perversely discourage cashing out.
C. Test on seeded setup states, not only empty boards

Create a small diagnostic suite containing:

    A clean one-deep precursor;
    Two- and three-row partial setups;
    A Tetris-ready well;
    A ready well with a delayed I;
    A nearly blocked well;
    A high stack requiring abandonment of the plan.

Then measure:

    Does it build from a precursor?
    Does it preserve a useful setup?
    Does it take a reachable Tetris?
    Does it recover when continuing to build is unsafe?

These separate acquisition, maintenance, cash-out, and recovery failures.

If the current scorer takes Tetrises from seeded wells but never builds them from empty, that points toward a horizon/incentive problem. If it rejects an immediately reachable Tetris, inspect the scoring and transition logic before training anything.

Seeded states also provide a natural training curriculum later.
D. Use multi-step rollouts with a style-capable continuation policy

Two-ply search cannot explicitly see most empty-board-to-Tetris plans. Longer planning is therefore a natural option.

On CPU, I would try sampled rollouts before a large general-purpose MCTS implementation:

    Generate candidate root moves.
    Simulate several future piece sequences.
    Continue with the simple well-building controller or dense-style policy.
    Measure Tetrises, recovery behavior, and failure.
    Compare candidates on the same sampled sequences to reduce variance.

Crucially, the continuation policy must know how to build wells. Rolling out the flat-stacking champion may simply dismantle every promising setup and conclude that setup moves have no value.

Respect the information available to the bot: beyond the actual preview, sample future pieces from the appropriate randomizer distribution. Do not let search inspect the evaluator’s hidden future tape.

You can trigger this more expensive planning only when:

    Starting or abandoning a well;
    Choosing between competing setup plans;
    Handling a drought;
    Approaching a risk threshold.

That is likely cheaper than deeper search on every placement.
4. Training becomes useful after you have a style signal

The decoupled-head direction is reasonable once its targets describe style. Training it on survival-teacher disagreements does not do that.

Good target sources include:

    Actions from the explicit well-building controller;
    Better actions identified by style-aware rollouts;
    Human demonstrations in the same ruleset;
    Returns containing actual Tetris rewards and failure penalties;
    Pairwise comparisons of trajectories for human-facing style.

For distillation, I would use dataset aggregation: let the student play, label the states it actually reaches with the teacher, and repeat. A frozen bank from another policy is a weak test of imitation learning.

For reinforcement learning, use a genuine task reward. The historical update

[ z(s)\leftarrow\gamma z(s')+(1-\gamma)z(s) ]

does not become a task-grounded Bellman target merely by describing its second term as a placement reward. It contains no independent evidence that a Tetris is desirable.

A style critic could instead learn:

[ V_{\text{style}}(s) \leftarrow r_{\text{style}}(s,a,s') +\gamma V_{\text{style,target}}(s'). ]

Potential-based shaping,

[ r'=r+\eta[\gamma\Phi(s')-\Phi(s)], ]

is another option for giving intermediate setup feedback while retaining an explicit task reward. Its usual policy-invariance guarantee requires the standard discounting and terminal-handling conditions.

The earlier failures justify caution with your old absolute-survival recipe. They do not establish that ordinary reward-based RL is unsuitable for this task.
5. Other worthwhile options
Optimize a small heuristic directly

For perhaps 5–15 style weights, random search, the cross-entropy method, or CMA-ES may be more productive than training 430K parameters.

Optimize for Tetrises under a survival constraint, using paired piece tapes and staged evaluation. This could discover a useful teacher with comparatively little training machinery.
Add representation features later, with an exact neutral initialization

If you do try extra input channels, copy the original weights and initialize the new-channel convolution weights to zero. Subject to unchanged surrounding computation, this preserves the original function at initialization.

That is a cleaner starting point than using C3-style affine fitting as a “neutral warm-up”: C3 explicitly changed rankings and behavior.

A residual style branch with zero initial output offers the same useful baseline contract.
Speed up search with valid pruning

The proposed

[ v_1+\gamma(\max v_2-\operatorname{mean}v_2) ]

does not generally make expanded and unexpanded candidates comparable. It measures the best successor’s advantage over its siblings, not the absolute quality of the continuation. A uniformly excellent successor set and a uniformly terrible one can receive similar centered bonuses.

Safer choices include:

    Fully expand shortlisted roots and choose only among those expanded roots;
    Use a cheap style-aware proposal policy;
    Cache duplicate leaf evaluations;
    Distill a smaller scorer once you have a worthwhile teacher.

Make shortlist selection diverse enough to retain setup moves, rather than filtering exclusively by the flat-stacking value.
6. Change the style metrics too

Lines per 100 pieces is almost conserved during stable survival. Four cells enter per piece and ten leave per cleared line:

[ L=\frac{4P+B_{\text{start}}-B_{\text{end}}}{10}. ]

With bounded board occupancy, the long-run rate approaches 40 lines per 100 pieces regardless of whether those lines are singles or Tetrises.

Use:

    Tetrises per 100 pieces;
    Fraction of cleared lines belonging to Tetrises;
    Existing Tetris share of clear events;
    Setup-to-Tetris conversion rate;
    Time between Tetrises;
    Height and recovery statistics;
    Long-horizon failure rate.

For human training, also inspect whether the bot preserves understandable plans and uses realistically executable input sequences. Tetris rate alone is not the whole playstyle objective.
Recommended revised order

    Make zero-style evaluation exactly reproduce the full-two-ply champion.
    Add seeded well-building/cash-out/recovery diagnostics.
    Try a designated-well controller and dense precursor features.
    Add sampled multi-step planning with style-capable continuations.
    Train or distill from the first demonstrably stylish teacher.
    Change representation if fitting/generalization diagnostics justify it.

The central missing ingredient is a signal that values the path to a Tetris. Your experiments have mostly rewarded completed or nearly completed setups while collecting data from a policy that avoids constructing them. I would address that mismatch before redesigning what the network can see.
