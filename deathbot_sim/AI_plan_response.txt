Bottom line

I would move into the demo phase now, while leaving room for one tightly bounded, inference-only style experiment. I would not start another general training campaign.

That is a resource-allocation recommendation—not a conclusion that the policy has reached its theoretical limits. The project has demonstrated three useful assets:

    A substantially improved 1-ply network: fullsplit_seed42.
    A strong survival search policy, with a practical speedup through b8_diverse.
    A modest but sustained style preference, plus evidence that switching back to survival play can rescue at least some dangerous style-generated positions.

The remaining research opportunities are real, but most require a new source of information or a meaningfully different control formulation. Repeating training, increasing λ, or adding another small sweep is unlikely to produce a large improvement.

The forced-well idea has merit primarily as a reversible inference-time constraint or training curriculum. “Force wells, then distill survival back into the network” is much less well-supported.
1. What I would—and would not—conclude from the results

A few distinctions matter for the decision.
The 1-ply improvement is genuine; its proposed mechanism is not established

The +468-piece promotion result is strong evidence that search-to-network training worked.

However, §36’s explanation that this was necessarily a “one-time trajectory-smoothing event” goes beyond the evidence. That student was trained against a stronger, different policy—not merely its own transformed outputs. A second round failing to improve significantly does not establish that the first round contained no useful teacher information.

Similarly, the extension’s +42.5 with (t=0.69) supports “not worth pursuing at this budget,” not “the true effect is zero.” The former is sufficient reason to stop.
Full-network style distillation has not actually been ruled out

§37.4 overstates the negative evidence:

    The earlier style students were small feature-based heads.
    E2 tested survival-policy proposals.
    E3 continued survival-teacher distillation.

Those are not three failures of full-network imitation of a strong stylish teacher. Nor do they establish that a decoupled head is the only acceptable architecture. Freezing survival weights also does not make the resulting combined policy survival-safe.

This leaves a legitimate research opportunity—but not one I would pursue before establishing a teacher whose style is worth copying.
The rescue result is encouraging, but narrower than “forced states are recoverable”

The seven successful handoffs are seven starting points on one death trajectory, including several low-occupancy positions. They establish an important existence result: the survival policy can actively recover from that trajectory, including a late state.

They do not establish that:

    occupancy 90 is generally a timely intervention threshold;
    λ20-generated positions are similarly recoverable;
    hard-forced nine-column stacks are recoverable;
    the controller will recognize danger before the recovery window closes.

Those questions need direct testing if forcing is made more aggressive.
2. Is the current style actually sufficient for the intended demo?

This is worth deciding before doing more optimization.

The sustained λ10 results are roughly 0.15–0.22 Tetrises per 100 pieces: approximately one Tetris every 450–670 placements. At stable throughput, that means only roughly 1.5–2.2% of cleared lines are cleared in Tetrises.

That is a convincing improvement over the baseline, but it is not yet recognizable, sustained Tetris stacking in the ordinary human sense.

Consequently:

    For a demo of “a learned evaluator plus search surviving 20G,” you already have enough.
    For a demo of “a bot demonstrating good Tetris-oriented Death stacking,” the current style may still fall short.
    For human training, legibility, reachable placement choices, and recovery explanations may currently be more valuable than the small Tetris-rate increase.

I would inspect representative, unselected recordings and make that product decision explicitly. A sixfold improvement over an almost-zero baseline can sound more impressive than it looks.
3. Where the forced-well idea has real weight

There are three different proposals hiding under that name.
A. Increase λ further

This is the least interesting version.

It is cheap to test, but the λ20 result already suggests that stronger pressure along the existing potential can increase inventory and risk without producing much additional sustained Tetris throughput.

I would not infer a universal ceiling of 0.2 Tetrises/100 from the current runs—deaths and policy dynamics complicate that interpretation—but the evidence does not make λ30/40 an especially promising next investment.
B. Reserve a well as a temporary strategic commitment

This is meaningfully different, and has not been cleanly tested by the existing negatives.

A scalar potential says:

    “Prefer this setup, unless another move scores better.”

A well-preservation constraint says:

    “While pursuing this plan, choose the best survival move among placements that preserve it.”

That distinction matters. A shallow evaluator can repeatedly abandon a beneficial long-term plan because each individual abandonment looks locally attractive. A temporary constraint can prevent those reversals without requiring the value function to understand the entire future payoff.

Also, the NN-free controller’s failure does not rule this out. There is a substantial difference between:

    choosing placements by a hand-coded well potential; and
    using the existing neural/search survival evaluation inside a constrained set of placements.

The latter retains the project’s strongest capability.

However, merely reserving a column is insufficient. In 20G, the well must be reachable under ARS, and the surrounding stack must remain serviceable. A geometrically open column is not automatically a usable I channel.
C. Force wells, then retrain to regain survival

This is plausible as a curriculum, but there is no automatic restoration mechanism.

The crucial question is: what produces the labels that teach safer well stacking?

    Imitating the forced policy can reproduce its mistakes.
    Imitating the flat survival policy on forced states can teach the student to dismantle the well.
    Regressing toward the student’s own outcomes recreates familiar weak-target problems.

The missing ingredient is not simply more well-containing boards. It is better decisions on those boards that preserve useful style while avoiding unnecessary risk.

A risk-controlled style teacher, successful recovery trajectories, or counterfactual continuations could provide that information. Forcing alone does not.

If a controller’s mode or chosen well changes its preferred action, that information also needs to be available to the student; otherwise the imitation task can contain conflicting targets.
4. The one remaining style experiment I would consider high-value

I would favor reversible well commitment with survival fallback, rather than another distillation attempt.

A minimal version would be:

    BUILD/PRESERVE: designate one well and use the existing survival scorer to rank placements that preserve a usable channel and reasonable outside-well structure.
    CASH OUT: take an appropriate reachable Tetris, then reassess the plan.
    RECOVER: drop the commitment when risk becomes excessive or acceptable preserving moves disappear.
    Re-enter style only after recovery, using hysteresis to avoid oscillation.

This is not the old NN-free controller: the NN/search still performs the placement selection.

Two implementation details are important:

    Do not apply the constraint only after a survival top-k shortlist has discarded the relevant setup moves. Initially use full root expansion, or explicitly retain well-preserving candidates. The bonus-free shortlist results do not establish stylish-candidate coverage.
    Treat occupancy thresholds and value margins as heuristics, not certified safety conditions. Forced stacking can create dangerous geometry at relatively low occupancy.

Cheapest preliminary check

Before building that controller, the already available λ20 + rescue variant is a sensible low-cost test. It asks whether stronger style pressure becomes useful when its accumulation failure is interrupted.

But run the actual switching policy on the known death tape—not just manual handoffs—and inspect whether it intervenes in time. Then test a small, fixed set of additional tapes.

I would compare a few fixed policies, not launch a broad parameter search:

    λ10 reference;
    λ10 with rescue;
    λ20 with the same rescue rule.

If aggressive style mostly creates a cycle of build backlog → rescue by singles → rebuild backlog, it has not solved the style problem. Measure Tetris conversion and time between Tetrises, not only occupancy and style-off fraction.

Stop if the result is not visibly or quantitatively material. Another small improvement over 0.18 Tetrises/100 would not justify reopening the whole campaign.
5. Are there unexplored paths for better 1-ply survival?

Yes, but I do not see an obvious remaining cheap one with sufficiently strong expected value.

The credible research directions would involve something different from continued hard-argmax CE:

    More structured preview-aware learning: predicting useful properties of the next-piece continuation, rather than asking the network to recover everything through one scalar ranking loss.
    Counterfactual training concentrated on consequential mistakes: using actual survival outcomes or recovery quality to distinguish important errors from harmless teacher disagreements.
    A different training distribution and objective: particularly successful risk-controlled style trajectories, if fast stylish play becomes the primary goal.

Those avenues are not invalidated by the existing experiments. But they require additional target design, labeling, diagnostics, and validation. They are research projects, not obvious next patches.

The successful fullsplit result also warns against using offline imitation fidelity as a hard gameplay gate. Conversely, that lesson does not imply that every modest offline change deserves another large evaluation.

For now, I would retain fullsplit_seed42 as the 1-ply champion. If its practical demo performance is adequate, there is little reason to spend heavily on another uncertain increment.
6. What I would do in the demo phase

The most valuable remaining engineering work is making the demonstrated policy reliably be the displayed and executed policy.

My priorities would be:

    Resolve the visualizer divergence.
    Use a shared, explicit piece tape and stop at the first difference in board, candidate set, score, or action. E0 shows why “same seed” is insufficient. Do not assume the remaining issue is candidate ordering before tracing it.

    Support canonical recorded replays.
    Store the piece sequence, chosen placement, input path, board transitions, and complete policy configuration. Offline search can then be displayed at the desired speed without maintaining a second decision implementation.

    Audit execution at the intended TAP timing.
    Static reachability and a BFS input path are not, by themselves, an end-to-end demonstration of execution under the intended input and timing conditions.

    Make the plan visible.
    Display the designated well, build/recover mode, and a short reason for abandonment or recovery. This could improve the human-training value more than another small numerical style gain.

    Match claims to the actual configuration and horizon.
    The long-horizon stylish results, shortened-beam survival results, and rescue training-seed results are different pieces of evidence. Do not silently combine them into a claim that the final composite policy has already passed all those tests.

Recommendation

Proceed with the demo. Do not restart broad training.

If stronger Tetris-oriented behavior is essential to the demo, reserve a small, explicit budget for force/commit + rescue, beginning with the existing λ20-plus-hysteresis machinery. That is the best remaining near-term opportunity because it exploits capabilities already demonstrated and changes the decision structure rather than merely repeating training.

Keep full-network style distillation and longer-horizon style planning on the future roadmap. They remain scientifically open—but they should follow a teacher that makes consistently worthwhile plans, not be expected to manufacture that teacher from forced trajectories after the fact.
